# Improvement claims

For each material improvement claimed in the review request, PR title/body, or changed documentation, record the claim and its scope in the existing review notes. Cover favorable claims as well as suspected defects; absence of a benchmark is a reason to investigate, not to skip the claim. Behavioral claims need targeted tests; passing correctness tests alone does not establish faster execution, lower memory use, or better model quality.

- Bind evidence to the exact baseline and reviewed revisions, representative workload/data, metric, commands, dependency/runtime versions, hardware and relevant settings (for example batch size, precision and thread count). Explain whether available hardware represents the claimed deployment; do not extrapolate CPU simulation to GPU throughput or one device to all devices.
- Inspect supplied tests and measurements, then run feasible bounded reproductions under the workflow's existing execution permissions. Keep author-provided results attributed; do not call them independently reproduced. The parent owns execution when reviewers are inspection-only. Preserve correctness checks and material tradeoffs alongside the claimed improvement.
- For numerical claims, set the comparison method and practical tolerance before interpreting results. Use comparable baseline/candidate workloads and settings, appropriate warmup and accelerator timing/synchronization, repeated samples and reported variability, not a single best run. Retain raw results, sample counts, summary statistic and effect size; assess whether the approximate magnitude claimed is supported within measurement uncertainty. No universal percentage tolerance establishes fairness for every metric.
- Local hardware may be shared. Record observed CPU/GPU activity, memory pressure or swapping, I/O contention and thermal/power conditions when relevant and observable; mark unavailable observations. Do not assume an idle or isolated machine. Run baseline and candidate sequentially in paired, alternating or randomized order to limit drift; avoid overlapping competing benchmarks or other agent-owned heavy checks on the same resources. Never stop unrelated processes or alter system resource controls without existing authorization.
- Bound repetitions and reruns before measurement. More samples do not remove systematic contention or thermal bias. If variability, drift or unequal resource pressure could explain the claimed effect, report the measurement as inconclusive; do not confirm or refute the claim from that run. Retain noisy attempts and any exclusion criteria; never cherry-pick favorable samples. A quieter rerun or representative dedicated hardware is the next check when the bounded local protocol cannot resolve the effect.
- For each claim, conclude supported, contradicted or unverified, with evidence provenance and tested scope. Supported means the evidence supports that scope; identify separately whether independent reproduction succeeded. Missing relevant hardware or unresolved noise leaves the claim unverified with the exact prerequisite, owner and next check. Feasible work not yet run remains incomplete. Contradiction requires reliable counterevidence, not a noisy slowdown or failure to reproduce. Missing verification alone is not a source defect or grounds for rejection; carry it into verification readiness and confidence under the existing decision rules.

Retain this evidence in the existing notes and check artifacts; no new result schema is required. Pass the relevant claims and protocol to the selected reviewers, reconcile their conclusions during candidate validation, and summarize unresolved claim limits in the final review.
